Selected work

Travel Tech · Via Indonesia

Fixing what travelers see — and how fast they see it

Search was slow, and the system promised luggage that wasn't in the ticket. I fixed the top two issues and upgraded the GDS integrations behind them.

GDS integrationSearch performanceData accuracy

The challenge

When I joined, the platform's number one and number two issues were clear: flight search took far too long, and ticket benefits displayed incorrectly. A fare without checked luggage could show "23 kg included" — because benefits had been hardcoded into the system years earlier instead of being read from the airlines and GDS. For a travel product, showing a customer luggage they don't actually have isn't a cosmetic bug; it's a trust and complaints problem.

What I did

I tackled both from the integration layer. We upgraded the connections to the GDS systems (Sabre, Galileo Travelport) and reworked benefit display to come from live API returns instead of hardcoded values — so what the airline says is what the traveler sees. Alongside the search-time and accuracy fixes, I owned the annual roadmap with management, wrote the PRDs and BRDs, coordinated design, engineering, legal and compliance, and tested features myself before every release.

What came out of it

Faster search, accurate fare benefits straight from the source, and booking integrations the business could rely on. After the fix went live, complaints about incorrect benefits dropped by around 60%.

Fintech · Komunal

Automating the loan pipeline

Credit scoring was solid — but manual. I turned it into an automated workflow and opened it up to outside lenders via API.

Workflow automationOpen APIB2B2C

The challenge

Komunal's credit scoring was already good — the bottleneck was that the process around it ran manually. Loan requests from SME owners had to be picked up, tracked and moved through analysis by hand, which capped how fast (and how many) loans could flow through.

What I did

I designed the automated flow: the moment an SME owner submits a loan request, it lands in the credit analysts' system as a to-do, and every progress change updates automatically — so analysts spend their time analysing, not administrating, and borrowers wait less. I also built a flow mechanism that stores every submitted document neatly along the way, so nothing gets lost between submission and decision. On top of that, I worked on an open API so interested lenders and financing partners could plug in and run their own loan products through the platform.

What came out of it

Shorter waiting times for borrowers, a tidier document trail for analysts, a platform other lenders could build on — and a contribution to growth in monthly loan disbursement.

Agritech · Koltiva

Traceability from farm gate to global brand

Palm oil, seaweed, rubber, coconut, cocoa — I made sure the apps worked where it mattered most: in the field.

B2B SaaSField opsDigital payments

The challenge

Koltiva's platforms trace commodities — palm oil, seaweed, rubber, coconut, cocoa — from smallholder farms into global supply chains. But software that works in a demo doesn't automatically work on a farm with patchy signal and users who've never used an app like it. My job was making sure it actually ran well in the field.

What I did

I didn't manage this from a desk — I went to the field myself. On our GSMA project, we digitalised payments for farmers and commodity traders in rural Indonesia: farmers could buy products through the platform and transact their harvest to traders directly in the app — which also helped them obtain a fairer price for their results. Separately, I worked on the Nestlé Global application — a track-and-trace platform for sources of dairy, coffee and cocoa, planned to roll out to Nestlé companies worldwide. I drove the initiation phase through to the working prototype and winning the tender, before handing it over to a colleague for delivery.

What came out of it

The farmer base tripled during the project, and 80% of farmers went on to transact digitally — rural farmers and traders trading at fairer prices, a won global tender for one of the world's largest food companies, and two promotions in two years, from Officer to Lead to Manager.

Automotive · Mercedes-Benz Indonesia

Solving the case of the missing parts

Assemblies were stalling halfway because parts never arrived. I traced the problem back to the warehouse — and redesigned it.

LeanLayout planningLogistics

The challenge

Assemblies kept stalling mid-build. An operator would be 45% — sometimes 80% — through an assembly before discovering a required part simply wasn't there. Each discovery meant a stopped line, a search, and a production delay.

What I did

Instead of treating it as an assembly problem, I traced it upstream. The investigation showed the missing parts had never been sent from the warehouse in the first place. So I analysed the warehouse's storage and picking flows and redesigned the layout to improve capacity and accessibility — making parts easier to locate, pick and dispatch completely before assembly ever starts. The work became my bachelor's thesis.

What came out of it

Fewer mid-assembly surprises and a warehouse organised around how parts actually flow to the line — plus my first real lesson in root-cause thinking: the problem you see is rarely where the problem lives.
